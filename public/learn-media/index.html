01. Keep both sides balanced

Adding 7 to both sides of x − 7 = 12 preserves equality and gives x = 19.

GIFMP4Still
Sequence and model limits

A level balance carries x minus 7 on one pan and 12 on the other. Plus 7 is added equally to both pans. The balance stays level as the equation simplifies to x equals 19.

The balance is an analogy for equality, not a dynamic scale simulation.

math/algebra 11.2 seconds

02. Run the formula backward

Multiply σ = F/A by A, then divide by σ. With F = 12,000 N and σ = 150 N/mm², A = 80 mm².

GIFMP4Still
Sequence and model limits

Four stages rearrange the stress formula: sigma equals force over area; sigma times area equals force; area equals force over sigma; the substituted result is 80 square millimetres. A final substitution checks the original equation.

Assumes nonzero area and a positive specified allowable stress. Reaching an allowable is not approval of a complete structural design.

math/ratios-units 8.8 seconds

03. Let the units cancel

240 mm × (1 in / 25.4 mm) leaves inches; the rounded result is 9.45 in.

GIFMP4Still
Sequence and model limits

The conversion factor has inches above millimetres. The millimetres in the original measurement and denominator are struck through together, leaving 9.45 inches.

1 inch = 25.4 mm exactly. The display rounds 240/25.4 to two decimal places for teaching.

04. Square the conversion factor

A square 1 m on each side contains 100 × 100 square centimetres: 1 m² = 10,000 cm².

GIFMP4Still
Sequence and model limits

A one-metre square is subdivided into a 100-by-100 grid. The equation changes from the length conversion to the squared conversion, showing 10,000 square centimetres.

The fine grid represents 100 centimetres along each side; stronger grid lines mark every tenth division.

05. Size changes faster than you think

A geometrically similar object at twice every length has four times the area and eight times the volume.

GIFMP4Still
Sequence and model limits

A square and cube grow from scale factor one to two. The final square is divided into four equal squares, and the visible cube faces into a two-by-two grid. Labels show area times four and volume times eight.

Mass also scales by eight only when density remains unchanged.

math/graphs 9.6 seconds

06. Slope and intercept

For y = 2x + b, each unit of run adds two units of rise. Moving b shifts the line without changing the slope.

GIFMP4Still
Sequence and model limits

A point travels along y equals 2x plus 3 while its rise and run are traced. Then the intercept increases to 5 and the whole line moves upward, retaining slope 2.

The axes use arbitrary numerical units; the graph is a mathematical line, not measured sensor data.

07. Split a force into components

At 35° above +x, a 500 N force has Fx ≈ 410 N and Fy ≈ 287 N. Their vector sum is still the original force.

GIFMP4Still
Sequence and model limits

A 500-newton arrow rotates from horizontal through 35 degrees to vertical. Its horizontal and vertical projections change, but the reconstructed magnitude stays at 500 newtons.

Angle is measured counterclockwise from +x. Component values are rounded to whole newtons.

math/triangles-vectors 11.2 seconds

08. A radian is a radius of arc

Radians are arc length divided by radius. One radian has an arc as long as the radius; π radians is half a turn.

GIFMP4Still
Sequence and model limits

A radius sweeps around a circle and highlights the intercepted arc. The animation pauses at one radian, pi radians, and two pi radians, showing the corresponding angles in degrees.

The circle demonstrates angle geometry; the sweep timing is instructional.

physics/contact 9.6 seconds

09. Static friction matches the push

With N = 100 N and μs = 0.40, static friction can match a push up to 40 N. After slip, μk = 0.30 gives 30 N friction.

GIFMP4Still
Sequence and model limits

A crate stays still as equal opposing push and static-friction arrows grow toward 40 newtons. A 50-newton push causes sliding; kinetic friction is 30 newtons, leaving 20 newtons net to the right.

Ideal dry Coulomb friction with prescribed normal load and no other horizontal forces. Motion is schematic rather than a calibrated displacement-time plot.

physics/elastic 10.4 seconds

10. Same load, more area, less stress

Under 12,000 N axial tension, areas of 40, 80 and 160 mm² give average stresses of 300, 150 and 75 MPa.

GIFMP4Still
Sequence and model limits

An end-view cross-section grows while the axial load stays fixed. The displayed stress falls inversely with area: 300 to 150 to 75 megapascals.

Uniform average axial stress, σ = F/A. Does not include stress concentrations, bending, buckling, or a material allowable.

physics/bending 9.6 seconds

11. Beam depth and bending stiffness

For equal width, length, material and tip load, doubling rectangular beam depth gives 8× I and one-eighth of the deflection.

GIFMP4Still
Sequence and model limits

Two cantilever beams receive equal growing tip loads. The 2-millimetre-deep beam deflects 13.50 millimetres; the 4-millimetre-deep beam deflects 1.69 millimetres. The visible bending is exaggerated.

Linear elastic, slender, small-deflection cantilevers. E = 200 GPa, L = 300 mm, b = 25 mm, F = 5 N, h = 2 or 4 mm. Visual thickness and bending are enlarged for clarity.

physics/potential 9.6 seconds

12. Trade height for speed

For a 2 kg frictionless slider dropping 2 m from rest, 39.24 J of potential energy becomes kinetic energy; the final speed is about 6.26 m/s.

GIFMP4Still
Sequence and model limits

A non-rotating slider moves down a straight ramp. Its potential-energy bar shrinks as its kinetic-energy bar grows by the same amount. Total mechanical energy stays at about 39.2 joules.

No friction, drag, rolling energy or other losses. Uses g = 9.81 m/s². The playback timeline is slowed for teaching.
