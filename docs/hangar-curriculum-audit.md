# Hangar editorial and curriculum verification audit

Date: October 1, 2026. Baseline: `568beedb126543d7ae8c52c0c0f0cf39a9f4f657`, including the Hangar additions in #45 and the capstone follow-up in #46.

## Verdict and scope

**NOT CERTIFIED.** This change delivers an editorial pass on all five Hangar missions, independent closed-form checks of their documented beam idealizations, and an exhaustive inventory with automated screening of the current lessons and registered benches. It does not establish independent scientific correctness of every claim, worked example, answer, animation or simulation, and it is not a safety, airworthiness or manufacturing approval.

The inventory discovers all items rather than certifying a sample by extrapolation: 166 lessons, 664 lesson-check questions, 172 registered benches (19 without current lesson assignments), 17 tracks, five Hangar missions, and 48 model/helper modules with exported functions at this baseline. Counts are generated by `scripts/curriculum-audit.mjs` and may change as content is added. Every item carries explicit remaining verification requirements. A direct test-file mention is traceability, not a measurement of code coverage.

## Substantive corrections

### Full span is not always half span

The plate section helper previously used half the specified span for every plate. That convention is appropriate only for the full-span, lift-fed glider wing. A standalone fin's span is its root-to-tip distance; a supported payload plate's span is its rail-to-rail distance. The error halved the bending stress and divided calculated deflection by eight in the new missions.

The solver now retains the wing convention and uses the full stated span for standalone plates. The beam benchmarks are calculated independently from the elementary section and support equations. These equations are valid within the stated linear-elastic, small-deflection idealization, not as physical predictions after that idealization fails.

| Starting component | Corrected bending stress | Corrected linear sag | Decision under the existing classroom limits |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Fin, 100 × 60 × 1 mm printed PLA, 10 N tip load | 100 MPa | 317.46 mm | Fails strength, sag and shop wall rule; sag is outside the model domain |
| Half-spar, 450 mm long, 8 mm OD, 0.8 mm wall aluminum, 6 N tip load | 90.98 MPa | 22.28 mm | Strength passes; sag fails |
| Payload plate, 200 × 120 × 2 mm printed PLA, 30 N center load | 18.75 MPa | 29.76 mm | Safety factor 1.6 fails the required 2; sag also fails and is outside the model domain |

A 10%-of-span warning is an explicitly labeled classroom screen. It is not a universal accuracy guarantee for deflections below that number. Large calculated deflections are presented as evidence that the simple model is outside its domain, not as an actual deformed shape.

### Achievable revisions without weakening the brief

The original mass, cost, safety-factor and sag limits remain unchanged. Independent tests include an achievable revision within the current sliders for every mission:

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Glider | CG at 150 mm | 1.75 mm | 42.32 g, all modeled parts | USD 64.73, wing only |
| Drone arm | Same section in aluminum | 1.14 mm | 21.38 g | USD 19.34 |
| Water rocket fin | 60 mm span, 40 mm chord, 3.5 mm thickness, printed PLA | 2.40 mm | 10.42 g | USD 27.39 |

These are classroom model examples, not build recommendations. A shorter fin still needs aerodynamic assessment; a shorter rail spacing still needs to fit the real mounting layout. Neither is verified by a structural pass.

### Editorial and presentation changes

The new mission briefs now define their loads, dimensions, units, assumptions, material knockdowns, budget scope and unmodeled behavior. Unsupported launch-pressure/thrust claims were removed from the water-rocket exercise because that system is not simulated. The payload's supplied 6 g factor is identified as the total load factor, not an extra factor to add to gravity. Its 500 g load is separated from the plate-only mass budget.

Copied glider-test and nylon-molding explanations were replaced with module-appropriate text. Fin and payload visuals now show their own supports, full spans and loads instead of the drone tube. Cost is explicitly a primary-part quote, not an assembled-vehicle estimate. A price-ranked, material-compatible process is not necessarily geometrically feasible.

### Simulation and evidence integrity

- Invalid dimensions, nonfinite inputs, bad references, absent parts, unsupported L2 fidelity, negative load magnitudes and invalid tube walls fail the implemented input screen instead of becoming apparently successful results.
- Missing process quotes cannot pass the cost check. A nonapplicable analysis does not earn a pass; unloaded structures do not become successful static-load tests.
- The L1 beam-frequency estimate uses the appropriate ideal support coefficient and the modeled half-wing mass. It explicitly omits attached motors/payload, joint flexibility and damping.
- Result identity includes the full input, environment, excitation, teaching material/process tables and model revision.
- Editing a design invalidates its earlier analysis/test flags; completed worker responses for older inputs are rejected. Repeatedly sealing the same unchanged iteration does not increase its count.
- Existing design geometry and written ledger history are retained; older cached analysis status is not treated as verification of the changed model.

### Materials grading correction found in the wider audit

The registered bonding prediction exercise previously graded silicon carbide as a categorical insulator and graphite against a generic network pack. The corrected exercise uses named-material reference properties: silicon carbide is a semiconductor, and graphite conducts well within its sheets while sliding comparatively easily between them. The question states the direction and non-oxidizing thermal environment. Prior scores from the incorrect bank are not presented as scores on the corrected bank; the old stored record is left intact.

This does not certify all legacy bonding explanations. In particular, blanket claims about melting of networks, diamond at extreme temperatures, and ductility inferred only from bond strength still need a complete claim-by-claim review.

## Certification gaps that remain

1. Every lesson needs a complete claim/example/feedback/figure ledger, with independent calculations and references for each entry. Schema presence and a passing authored test do not establish this.
2. Every simulation needs a defined input domain, governing equations, numerical-error assessment, boundary cases and independent benchmark evidence for all relevant modes. The new Hangar tests cover the documented elementary models, not physical validation.
3. Several models use illustrative material values, empirical coefficients, process prices, strength knockdowns and failure criteria without grade-specific evidence. These must remain teaching assumptions, not qualified allowables.
4. The virtual test reruns the same equations as the analysis. There are no corresponding physical measurements establishing validity of these mission designs. Coupled loads, joints, shear deformation, local failure, fatigue, aeroelastic behavior and real manufacturing capability require separate evidence.
5. Legacy visualizations, textual generalizations, all non-slider interactions, external media, assistive technologies and authenticated deployment require additional review. A schematic is not a validated deformation field.
6. Learner studies are needed before claiming the assessments establish transfer or the intended beginner-to-advanced outcomes.

`scripts/curriculum-audit.mjs --require-certification` intentionally exits unsuccessfully while those independent validation requirements remain unmet. It must not be weakened merely to obtain a green certification label.
